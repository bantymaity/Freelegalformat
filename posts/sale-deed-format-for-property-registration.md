You have spent years saving up, cutting back on expenses, and navigating the complex maze of home loan approvals. Finally, you have found your dream house. You pay the token advance to the seller, and they hand you a document titled *"Agreement to Sell"*. 

You sign it, transfer the rest of the funds, take the keys, and throw a massive housewarming party. You are now the proud owner of a new home, right?

**Wrong. Legally, you own absolutely nothing.**

This is the most dangerous and widespread misconception in the Indian real estate market. An *Agreement to Sell* (commonly known as a Baina Patra or Token Agreement) does NOT transfer the ownership of a property. Under **Section 54 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882**, the absolute ownership of an immovable property worth more than Rs. 100 can only be transferred through a registered **Sale Deed**.

Until a Sale Deed is drafted, stamped, signed by both parties, and officially registered before the Sub-Registrar of Assurances, the seller remains the legal owner of the property—even if you are living in it and have paid 100% of the money!

In this exhaustive, insider's guide to property law, we will break down the exact legal difference between an Agreement to Sell and a Sale Deed, expose the **1% TDS trap** that ruins many buyers, provide a **free, comprehensive Sale Deed format**, and walk you through the ultimate registration checklist.

### Agreement to Sell vs. Sale Deed: The Ultimate Difference

Do not confuse these two documents. They represent two completely different stages of a real estate transaction.

| Parameter | Agreement to Sell (ATS) | Sale Deed (Final Conveyance) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What is it?** | A promise that the property *will* be sold in the future upon fulfilling certain conditions (like loan approval). | The final, ultimate document that legally transfers the title and ownership to the buyer. |
| **Legal Status** | Does NOT transfer ownership. It merely creates a right to demand the sale in the future. | Instantly transfers absolute ownership to the buyer upon registration. |
| **Stamp Duty** | Very nominal (e.g., 0.1% or a flat fee like Rs. 500, depending on the state). | Extremely high. Usually 5% to 7% of the property’s total market value. |
| **When is it signed?** | At the beginning of the transaction, when giving the 10% or 20% booking token. | At the very end of the transaction, when paying the final 80% to 90% amount. |

### The 1% TDS Trap (Section 194-IA of the Income Tax Act)

If you are buying a property worth **Rs. 50 Lakhs or more**, pay very close attention to this rule. 

Many buyers simply write a cheque for the full amount to the seller. Fast forward two years, and the buyer receives a massive penalty notice from the Income Tax Department. Why? Because under **Section 194-IA**, it is the **Buyer's legal responsibility** to deduct 1% TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) from the total sale consideration and deposit it directly to the government using Form 26QB.

* **Example:** If you are buying a flat for Rs. 60 Lakhs, you MUST NOT pay Rs. 60 Lakhs to the seller. You must pay Rs. 59.4 Lakhs to the seller, and deposit the remaining Rs. 60,000 (1%) to the Income Tax Department on the seller's behalf. 
* If you fail to deduct this, the IT Department will recover the 1% tax, plus heavy interest and penalties, directly from **you** (the buyer), not the seller. Your Sale Deed draft must explicitly mention this TDS deduction.

### 5 Non-Negotiable Clauses Every Sale Deed Must Have

A strong Sale Deed is your only shield against future family disputes, bank claims, or legal fraud. Ensure your draft contains these five clauses:

1. **The Indemnity Clause:** The seller must legally promise to financially compensate (indemnify) the buyer if a third party (like a hidden brother, sister, or bank) suddenly claims a right over the property after the sale.
2. **Clear Title & Encumbrance Free:** A written declaration by the seller that the property is not mortgaged to any bank, has no pending electricity/water bills, and is not locked in a court stay order.
3. **Passing of Physical Possession:** The exact date and time the seller hands over the vacant physical possession and the original keys to the buyer.
4. **Consideration & Payment Schedule:** A detailed table listing every single NEFT UTR number, cheque number, and TDS deduction that makes up the total sale price.
5. **Handover of "Chain Documents":** A clause confirming that the seller has handed over all previous original Sale Deeds (the history of the property) to the new buyer.

### The Sub-Registrar Office (SRO) Registration Protocol

Printing the deed is only 50% of the job. To make it legally binding, you must complete the SRO registration:

1. **Calculate the Circle Rate:** Every state government fixes a minimum value for real estate in a specific area, called the Circle Rate (or Ready Reckoner Rate). The stamp duty must be calculated on the *Higher* of the two: The actual Sale Price OR the Circle Rate value.
2. **Buy e-Stamp Paper:** Pay the heavy stamp duty (e.g., 5% to 7%) online via the SHCIL portal or a designated bank and generate the e-Stamp paper.
3. **The Golden Rule of Payment:** **Never hand over the final Demand Draft or complete the NEFT transfer to the seller a week in advance.** The safest method is to hand over the final payment Demand Draft to the seller *physically inside the Sub-Registrar's office*, right before you both sign the register.
4. **Two Witnesses:** Bring two independent witnesses (friends or colleagues, not direct family beneficiaries) along with their original Aadhaar and PAN cards.
5. **Biometrics & Photos:** The Sub-Registrar will take digital photos and biometric thumbprints of the Buyer, Seller, and Witnesses. Once approved, the document is permanently archived in government records.
