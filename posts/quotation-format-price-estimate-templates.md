Whether you run a small business, manage a construction contracting firm, offer freelance digital services, or operate a trading wholesale enterprise in India, securing new client orders begins with a single critical commercial document: **The Business Quotation** (also known as a Price Estimate or Price Quote).

When a potential client or corporate buyer inquires about your goods, manufacturing rates, or professional services, sending a casual WhatsApp message with a rough price figure looks unprofessional and exposes your business to pricing disputes later on.

A well-structured commercial quotation establishes transparent pricing, specifies line-item deliverables, defines payment milestones, clarifies GST taxes, and sets validity timelines.

More importantly, in commercial law under the **Indian Contract Act, 1872**, a quotation acts as a formal **Legal Offer**. Once a client accepts and signs your quotation (or issues a formal Purchase Order against it), the quotation transforms into a legally binding contract.

In this comprehensive guide, we explain the difference between a Quotation, Proforma Invoice, and Tax Invoice, detail the 8 essential elements of a professional price estimate, provide **two free ready-to-use downloadable Quotation formats** (Service Provider Draft and Material/Product Supply Draft), explain GST calculation rules, and share strategies to convert quotes into paid deals.

### Quotation vs. Proforma Invoice vs. Tax Invoice: Know the Difference

Business owners often confuse these three commercial documents. Understanding where a Quotation fits in the sales lifecycle prevents accounting errors:

[1. QUOTATION (Price Offer)] ➔ [2. PROFORMA INVOICE (Pre-Payment Bill)] ➔ [3. TAX INVOICE (Final Demand)]


| Parameter | Business Quotation | Proforma Invoice | Tax Invoice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stage in Sales Cycle** | Initial inquiry stage (before work begins). | Order confirmed stage (for advance collection). | Post-delivery / post-service completion stage. |
| **Legal Nature** | Non-binding offer until accepted by buyer. | Preliminary bill for advance processing. | Legally binding tax document demand for payment. |
| **Accounting Entry** | No entry in accounting journals or ledger. | No entry in financial books. | Debited to customer account; recorded for GST filing. |
| **GST Implication** | Mentions estimated GST rates for clarity. | Mentions estimated GST for advance payment. | Mandatory official GST document for claiming Input Tax Credit (ITC). |

### 8 Mandatory Elements of a Professional Business Quotation

To win client trust and prevent scope creep during project execution, ensure your quotation contains these eight core elements:

1. **Header & Branding:** Your registered company name, brand logo, corporate registration number (GSTIN / Udyam Aadhaar), address, and official email/phone.
2. **Document Title & Unique Number:** Clearly labeled as "QUOTATION" or "PRICE ESTIMATE" with a unique reference tracking number (e.g., `QT-2026-089`).
3. **Client Information:** Client’s company name, contact person name, designation, billing address, and GSTIN (if applicable).
4. **Issue Date & Validity Period:** Date of issuance and explicit validity tenure (e.g., "Valid for 30 calendar days from date of issue").
5. **Itemized Scope of Work / Bill of Quantities (BOQ):** Detailed table breaking down items/services, HSN/SAC codes, quantities, unit prices, and line-item totals.
6. **GST & Tax Breakdown:** Clear division between base subtotal, applicable GST rate (CGST + SGST for intra-state, IGST for inter-state), and final gross total.
7. **Payment Milestones & Terms:** Advance deposit required (e.g., 40% advance, 60% upon delivery), bank payment details, and late payment penal interest.
8. **Client Acceptance Block:** Dedicated space for the client to sign, stamp, and date their formal acceptance.

### How a Signed Quotation Becomes a Legally Enforceable Contract

In commercial law, a quotation by itself is merely an **invitation to treat** or a **legal offer**. It becomes a binding contract when:

1. **Client Signs the Acceptance Block:** The client signs, stamps, and returns a copy of the quotation.
2. **Client Issues a Purchase Order (PO):** The client sends an official Purchase Order referencing your Quotation Number.
3. **Client Pays Advance Consideration:** The client transfers an advance deposit based on the quotation terms.

Once any of these three actions occur, the quotation terms (including cancellation penalties, scope limits, and payment deadlines) become legally enforceable in court under the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

### 4 Common Errors That Cause Clients to Reject Quotations

1. **Vague Line Items:** Writing generic descriptions like "Website Work - Rs. 50,000" without detailing specific deliverables leads to scope creep and client mistrust.
2. **Omitting Validity Expiry Dates:** Failing to state a validity period allows clients to accept a price quote six months later when material costs or labor rates have increased.
3. **Hidden Charges:** Adding unexpected delivery, handling, or tax charges at the final billing stage alienates clients. Always disclose all cost components in the initial quote.
4. **Missing HSN/SAC Codes:** For GST-registered businesses, omitting HSN/SAC codes prevents corporate buyers from verifying their eligible Input Tax Credit (ITC).
