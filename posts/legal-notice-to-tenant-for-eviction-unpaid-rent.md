It usually starts with a small, harmless excuse. Your tenant asks for five extra days to pay the rent because their salary was delayed. You agree—you want to be a reasonable landlord. 

Next month, they pay only half. By the fourth month, they stop answering your calls altogether, their 11-month agreement has expired, and when you finally knock on the door of your own flat, they calmly tell you: *"Adjust it from the security deposit. I will leave when I find another place."*

At that exact moment, almost every Indian landlord gets the same reckless advice from neighbors or local brokers: *"Just cut their electricity connection, shut off the water valve, or put your own padlock on the main gate while they are at work."*

**Stop right there. Do not touch a single switch or lock.**

In India, the moment you try to forcefully throw out even a non-paying, overstaying tenant without due process of law, the tables flip overnight. The tenant walks into the nearest police station and files a criminal complaint against **you** for wrongful restraint, criminal intimidation, and mischief under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023. Suddenly, you are fighting for anticipatory bail instead of getting your house back.

Evicting a stubborn tenant in India is not a test of physical strength—it is a test of **procedural precision**. Everything hinges on your very first legal strike: a meticulously drafted **Notice to Quit (Eviction Legal Notice)**.

In this practical, no-nonsense property law guide, we will bust the viral 2026 "nationwide rent rules" myth, explain the strict **Section 106 notice calculation**, warn you about the fatal **Section 113 Rent-Acceptance Trap** that gets eviction suits dismissed in court, and hand you **two ironclad Eviction Notice templates** ready for dispatch.

### The 2026 Reality Check: Which Eviction Law Applies to Your Property?

If you scroll through social media in 2026, you will see dozens of reels claiming that "new central rent rules" automatically let every landlord in India charge 4x rent from overstaying tenants. **That is only half true.**

Because land and housing are State subjects in India, rental eviction operates under a split legal system. Before sending your notice, check which bucket your property falls into:

| Legal Framework | Where It Applies (2026 Status) | Overstaying Penalty & Forum |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Model Tenancy Act (MTA) Framework** | Only in states that have formally notified MTA-aligned laws—currently **Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, and Assam**. | Landlords can claim **2x monthly rent** for the first 2 months of overstay, and **4x monthly rent** for every subsequent month through the dedicated **Rent Court**. |
| **Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (Section 106)** | Applies nationwide to residential/commercial leases outside Rent Control protection (e.g., standard urban flats with market rent, or Delhi properties with rent above Rs. 3,500/month). | Requires a formal **Notice to Quit** terminating tenancy. After expiry, you file a civil ejectment suit and claim **Mesne Profits** (market-rate occupational damages under Order XX Rule 12 CPC). |
| **Old State Rent Control Acts** | Applies to protected tenancies in non-MTA states (e.g., Maharashtra Rent Control Act, Delhi Rent Control Act for rent up to Rs. 3,500/month, West Bengal Premises Tenancy Act). | You cannot evict at will. Your notice and suit must strictly cite statutory grounds—such as habitual rent default, illegal sub-letting, property damage, or *bona fide* personal need. |

*Practical takeaway:* Even if your state has not yet notified the Model Tenancy Act, a properly served **Section 106 Notice** combined with a strong claim for **Mesne Profits** is the exact legal mechanism that breaks a defaulting tenant's confidence.

### The Notice Period Rule: 15 Days vs. Your Written Agreement

Landlords routinely lose 6 to 12 months in civil court because they pick a random deadline like *"Vacate my flat within 7 days."* A defective notice gives the tenant's lawyer an easy technicality to stall your case.

Follow these three rules under **Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882**:

1. **Contract Beats Default:** Check your signed Rent Agreement or Leave and License Agreement first. If Clause 5 says *"Either party must give 30 days' (or 60 days') written notice,"* you **must** honor that 30-day or 60-day period.
2. **No Written Agreement (or Expired Lease):** If you have a verbal tenancy, or your 11-month agreement expired and the tenant stayed on as a month-to-month tenant, Section 106 mandates a minimum of **15 clear days' written notice** for residential or regular commercial premises (6 months applies only to agricultural or heavy manufacturing leases).
3. **When the Clock Starts:** The 15-day (or 30-day) clock starts running from the date the tenant **receives** the notice—not the date you printed or posted it. Always add a 3-to-4-day postal buffer when calculating your final eviction date.

### The Silent Case-Killer: Section 113 TPA (The "Acceptance of Rent" Trap)

This is the single most expensive mistake landlords make *after* sending an eviction notice—and almost nobody warns them about it.

Suppose you send a valid Legal Notice terminating the tenancy on December 31, 2026. The tenant ignores the deadline, stays inside your flat in January, and quietly transfers Rs. 20,000 into your bank account via UPI with the remark: *"January 2027 Rent."* You feel relieved to see the money and keep quiet.

**You may have just destroyed your own eviction notice.**

Under **Section 113 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (Waiver of Notice to Quit)**, if a landlord accepts rent for a period *after* the expiry of the quit notice in a way that shows an intention to treat the lease as continuing, the original eviction notice is legally treated as **waived (cancelled)**. You would have to serve a fresh notice all over again!

**The Courtroom Fix:**
If an overstaying tenant transfers money to your account after your notice expires, immediately send them an email and WhatsApp message stating:
> *"The sum of Rs. [Amount] transferred by you on [Date] is strictly NOT accepted as 'Rent' and does not renew or revive your terminated tenancy. It is received solely without prejudice towards part-payment of unauthorized occupational charges / Mesne Profits, and our eviction proceedings stand intact."*

This one paragraph protects your entire lawsuit under established Supreme Court jurisprudence (*Sarup Singh Gupta vs. S. Jagdish Singh*).

### Template 1: Legal Notice for Eviction & Recovery of Unpaid Rent Arrears (Section 106 TPA)

Use this draft when a tenant has stopped paying rent, violated agreement terms, or you simply want to terminate a month-to-month tenancy and reclaim your flat or shop.

> **STATUTORY LEGAL NOTICE FOR TERMINATION OF TENANCY, EVICTION & RECOVERY OF ARREARS**
> *(Under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882)*
>
> **BY REGISTERED POST WITH A.D. (RPAD) / SPEED POST & EMAIL**
>
> **Date:** 26th November 2026
>
> **TO:**
> **[Tenant's Full Legal Name]**
> [Complete Address of the Rented Flat / Shop / Premises]
> **Permanent / Native Address:** [Tenant's Permanent Address from Aadhaar, if available]
>
> **FROM:**
> **[Landlord's Full Legal Name]**
> [Landlord's Complete Residential Address]
> Mobile: [Your Number] | Email: [Your Email]
>
> **SUBJECT: Statutory Notice under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 for termination of tenancy, handover of vacant physical possession of [Flat/Shop No., Building Name, City], and recovery of unpaid rent arrears amounting to Rs. [Total Arrears]/-.**
>
> **Sir/Madam,**
>
> Under my own instructions as the lawful owner and landlord of the premises described below, I hereby serve upon you this formal Legal Notice:
>
> 1. That I am the absolute and lawful owner of the residential/commercial premises bearing **[Complete Property Address, Flat/Unit No., Floor, Building Name, City, PIN]** (hereinafter referred to as the **"Demised Premises"**).
> 2. That you were inducted as a tenant in the Demised Premises vide Rent Agreement dated **[Date of Agreement]** at a mutually agreed monthly rent of **Rs. [Monthly Rent]/-**, payable in advance on or before the **[e.g., 5th]** day of every English calendar month, excluding electricity, water, and society maintenance charges.
> 3. That in flagrant violation of the terms of the tenancy, you have willfully defaulted in paying the monthly rent since **[Month & Year of first default]**. As of the date of this Notice, you are in arrears of unpaid rent for **[Number]** months totaling **Rs. [Total Unpaid Rent]/-**, plus pending electricity/maintenance dues of **Rs. [Utility Dues]/-**.
> 4. That despite repeated polite reminders via phone calls and WhatsApp messages dated **[List 2–3 Dates]**, you have failed and neglected to clear the outstanding dues and have instead continued to occupy the Demised Premises unlawfully.
> 5. **Termination of Tenancy:** Please take formal notice that I no longer wish to continue your tenancy. By virtue of this Notice under Section 106 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882 (read with Clause **[Clause No.]** of our Rent Agreement), your tenancy in respect of the Demised Premises hereby stands **TERMINATED** upon the expiry of **[15 Days / 30 Days — match your Agreement]** from the date of your receipt of this Notice (or at the close of the current tenancy month ending on **[Insert Exact Date]**).
>
> **MY STATUTORY DEMANDS:**
> You are hereby called upon to comply with the following demands on or before **[Insert Exact Final Vacate Date]**:
>
> **A.** Hand over the peaceful, vacant, and physical possession of the Demised Premises in the same good condition in which it was handed over to you (subject to reasonable wear and tear), along with all original keys and fixtures.
> **B.** Clear the total outstanding rent arrears and utility dues of **Rs. [Total Due Amount]/-** via NEFT/RTGS/Demand Draft. *(Note: Any refundable security deposit of Rs. [Deposit Amount]/- held by me shall be adjusted strictly against unpaid arrears, pending utility bills, and verified physical damages at the time of handover, and the remaining balance, if any, shall be settled accordingly).*
>
> **WARNING OF MESNE PROFITS & LITIGATION:**
> Take serious note that if you fail to vacate the Demised Premises upon the expiry of this notice period, your occupation shall become wholly illegal and unauthorized. You shall thereupon be liable to pay **Mesne Profits / Unauthorized Occupational Damages** at the prevailing market rate of **Rs. [e.g., 2x Monthly Rent or Daily Rate of Rs. XXXX/-]** per month/day until actual handover, without prejudice to my right to institute an Eviction and Recovery Suit before the competent Civil Court / Rent Court entirely at your risk, cost, and consequence.
>
> Please note that any payment made by you after the expiry of this notice period shall be treated strictly as part-payment towards occupational damages/arrears and shall **never** be construed as a waiver of this notice or renewal of tenancy.
>
> **LANDLORD / SENDER:**
> *(Signature)*
> **[Your Full Legal Name]**

### Template 2: Eviction Notice on Expiry of an 11-Month Leave & License Agreement

If your tenant signed a **Leave and License Agreement** whose 11-month period has already ended (or is about to end) and they are refusing to leave—even if they are offering to pay rent—use this sharper, shorter format.

> **LEGAL NOTICE TO VACATE UPON EXPIRY OF LEAVE AND LICENSE AGREEMENT**
>
> **BY REGISTERED POST (RPAD) / SPEED POST & WHATSAPP/EMAIL**
>
> **Date:** 26th November 2026
>
> **TO:**
> **[Licensee's Full Legal Name]**
> [Complete Address of the Licensed Premises]
>
> **SUBJECT: Final Legal Notice demanding immediate vacant possession of [Property Address] upon efflux of time / expiry of Leave and License Agreement dated [Date].**
>
> **Sir/Madam,**
>
> 1. That you were granted a purely temporary, revocable permission to occupy my premises situated at **[Complete Address]** strictly as a Licensee under the Leave and License Agreement dated **[Agreement Date]** for a fixed term of **11 Months**, commencing from **[Start Date]** and expiring on **[Expiry Date]**.
> 2. That I had already intimated you in advance that I do not intend to renew or extend the said license beyond **[Expiry Date]**, as I require the premises for my own *bona fide* personal/family use.
> 3. That despite the automatic expiry of the Agreement by efflux of time under Section 111(a) of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, you have illegally failed to vacate the premises and have turned into an unauthorized trespasser/occupant.
>
> **DEMAND:**
> I hereby grant you one final opportunity of **15 (Fifteen) Days** from the receipt of this Notice to vacate the Licensed Premises, hand over the keys peacefully, and clear all electricity, water, and society maintenance bills up to the date of handover.
>
> Failing compliance within 15 days, I shall be constrained to file immediate eviction proceedings before the competent Authority / Civil Court and claim **liquidated overstay damages / Mesne Profits** at **[State contractual penalty rate, or 2x/4x monthly rent if in an MTA-notified state]** along with full litigation costs.
>
> Govern yourself accordingly.
>
> *(Signature)*
> **[Your Full Legal Name]**
> **[Contact Number]**

### The Dispatch Protocol: What If the Tenant Refuses to Accept the Envelope?

Many clever tenants see an advocate's or landlord's name on the postal envelope and tell the postman, *"Nobody by this name lives here"* or simply refuse to sign for it. Landlords panic, thinking the notice failed.

**Relax—Indian law anticipated this trick decades ago.**

Under **Section 106(4) of the Transfer of Property Act** and **Section 27 of the General Clauses Act**, if you dispatch a properly addressed notice via **Registered Post with Acknowledgement Due (RPAD)** or **India Post Speed Post**, and the postman marks the envelope as **"Refused"** or **"Unclaimed / Door Locked despite intimation"**, Indian courts presume **valid deemed service**.

**Follow this exact 4-step dispatch checklist so your proof holds up in court:**
1. **Print and Sign Two Copies:** Sign in blue ink on every page. Keep one exact photocopy for your court file.
2. **Send via RPAD / Speed Post:** Dispatch to the rented flat address *and* (if you have it from their KYC) their permanent native address.
3. **Never Open a Returned Envelope:** If India Post returns the envelope marked *"Refused"* or *"Unclaimed,"* **DO NOT tear it open.** Keep it completely sealed and attach the online India Post tracking sheet to it. The judge will open that sealed envelope in court as rock-solid proof that the tenant dodged service.
4. **Parallel Digital Service:** The same day you post the physical copy, email the signed PDF and send it to the tenant's registered WhatsApp number. Take a screenshot showing the blue ticks or delivery timestamp.

### What Happens If They Still Don't Leave?

In roughly 70% of residential cases, a formally worded legal notice mentioning **Mesne Profits** and **Civil Court recovery** shakes the tenant out of complacency—they realize staying longer will cost them far more than moving out, and they negotiate a handover date within two weeks.

If you are dealing with the stubborn 30% who refuse to budge after the notice period expires:
* **In MTA-Adopted States (TN, AP, UP, Assam):** File an eviction petition before the **Rent Court** (which is legally mandated to handle tenancy disputes swiftly rather than dragging them through traditional civil courts).
* **In Other States:** Engage a local civil/property advocate to file a **Suit for Ejectment, Arrears of Rent, and Mesne Profits** in the local Civil Court (or Rent Controller, if covered by a state Rent Control Act). During the trial, your advocate can also file an immediate application (under Order XV-A / Order XXXIX Rule 10 CPC) asking the judge to force the tenant to keep depositing monthly occupational charges in court while the case runs—taking away their incentive to stall the case.

### Conclusion

Owning a property and watching someone live in it rent-free is infuriating, and it is completely human to want to take matters into your own hands. Resist that impulse. Cutting utilities or locking doors hands a defaulting tenant a criminal weapon against you. Instead, fight smart: check your state's rental framework, calculate your notice period accurately, guard against the Section 113 waiver trap, and dispatch a sharp, legally compliant Eviction Notice via Speed Post. Once the paper trail is ironclad, the law works swiftly in the landlord's favor.
