### Unilateral vs. Mutual NDA: Which One Do You Need?

Before drafting the agreement, you need to understand the flow of information. NDAs generally fall into two broad categories:

**1. Unilateral NDA (One-Way):**
In this scenario, only one party is disclosing sensitive information, and the other party is receiving it and agreeing to keep it secret. 
*Example:* You are hiring a freelance graphic designer to create a logo for a secret product you are launching. Only *you* are sharing secrets. 

**2. Mutual NDA (Two-Way):**
Here, both parties are sharing confidential information with each other. 
*Example:* Two startups are discussing a potential partnership or merger. Both companies will look at each other's backend data and finances, so both need to be legally bound to stay quiet.

---

### 4 Critical Clauses Your NDA Must Have

Do not just copy-paste a random format from the internet without checking for these four specific clauses. A weak NDA is as bad as having no NDA at all.

1. **The Definition of "Confidential Information":** The agreement must clearly define what exactly is a secret. Is it the source code? The customer database? The marketing strategy? If you leave this vague, a court might rule that the leaked information wasn't officially "confidential."
2. **The Exclusions:** What is *not* confidential? Information that is already in the public domain (like your website's public URL) or information the receiver already knew before signing the NDA cannot be protected.
3. **The Term (Time Period):** NDAs do not last forever. In India, a standard NDA lasts between 2 to 5 years. You must specify how long the receiving party is bound to maintain secrecy after the project ends.
4. **Jurisdiction:** If the freelancer or agency breaches the contract and leaks your data, which city's court will handle the lawsuit? Always establish the jurisdiction in your home city (e.g., "Courts of Kolkata" or "Courts of Mumbai").

### Free Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) Formats

Here are two highly professional, legally vetted drafts. You can copy these, fill in your specific details, and execute them immediately.

#### Format 1: Unilateral NDA (Best for Hiring Freelancers, Agencies, or Employees)

> **NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT**
> 
> This Non-Disclosure Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into on this **[Date]**, by and between:
> 
> **[Your Company Name / Your Name]**, having its principal place of business at [Your Complete Address], hereinafter referred to as the **"Disclosing Party"**.
> 
> **AND**
> 
> **[Freelancer / Agency / Employee Name]**, having its principal place of business/residence at [Their Complete Address], hereinafter referred to as the **"Receiving Party"**.
> 
> **1. Purpose:**
> The Disclosing Party intends to share certain confidential and proprietary information with the Receiving Party for the purpose of [Mention the specific project, e.g., developing a digital storefront, software development, marketing consultation] (the "Purpose").
> 
> **2. Confidential Information:**
> "Confidential Information" shall mean any and all technical and non-technical information provided by the Disclosing Party, including but not limited to business plans, financial data, customer lists, source code, merchant tool integrations, algorithms, and marketing strategies, whether shared in writing, orally, or digitally.
> 
> **3. Obligations of Receiving Party:**
> The Receiving Party agrees to:
> a) Hold the Confidential Information in strict confidence and take all reasonable precautions to protect it.
> b) Not disclose any such Confidential Information to any third party without prior written consent.
> c) Use the Confidential Information solely for the "Purpose" mentioned in this Agreement and for no other intent.
> 
> **4. Exclusions:**
> This Agreement does not apply to any information that: 
> (i) was publicly known at the time of disclosure; 
> (ii) becomes publicly known through no fault of the Receiving Party; or 
> (iii) is required to be disclosed by a court of law.
> 
> **5. Term:**
> The obligations under this Agreement shall survive and continue for a period of **[e.g., 3 Years]** from the date of execution.
> 
> **6. Governing Law & Jurisdiction:**
> This Agreement shall be governed by the laws of India. Any disputes arising out of this Agreement shall be subject to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts at **[Your City, e.g., Kolkata / New Delhi]**.
> 
> IN WITNESS WHEREOF, the parties hereto have executed this Non-Disclosure Agreement as of the date first written above.
> 
> **Disclosing Party:** 
> Signature: _______________________
> Name: [Your Name]
> 
> **Receiving Party:**
> Signature: _______________________
> Name: [Receiver's Name]

#### Format 2: Mutual NDA (Best for B2B Partnerships & Co-Founders)

> **MUTUAL NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT**
> 
> This Mutual Non-Disclosure Agreement ("Agreement") is made on **[Date]**, between:
> 
> **Party A:** [Company Name A], located at [Address A]
> **Party B:** [Company Name B], located at [Address B]
> 
> (Party A and Party B are collectively referred to as the "Parties" and individually as a "Party").
> 
> **1. Purpose of Disclosure:**
> The Parties wish to explore a potential business relationship/partnership. During these discussions, each Party may disclose certain proprietary and confidential information to the other.
> 
> **2. Mutual Obligations:**
> Both Parties agree that the Receiving Party shall:
> a) Maintain the absolute confidentiality of the Disclosing Party’s information.
> b) Restrict access to the Confidential Information strictly to employees or legal advisors who have a "need to know" and are bound by similar confidentiality obligations.
> c) Return or permanently destroy all copies of the Confidential Information immediately upon written request from the Disclosing Party.
> 
> **3. Validity & Jurisdiction:**
> This Agreement remains valid for **[e.g., 5 Years]**. Any legal proceedings shall be subject to the jurisdiction of the courts in **[City Name]**, India.
> 
> **Signatures:**
> 
> For **[Party A Name]**: 
> Authorized Signatory: _______________________
> 
> For **[Party B Name]**: 
> Authorized Signatory: _______________________

### Does an NDA Need to be on Stamp Paper?

In India, a contract executed on plain white paper can be challenged in court regarding its validity. To make your NDA legally bulletproof, it must be printed on a **Non-Judicial Stamp Paper**. 

The value of the stamp paper varies by state. Usually, a stamp paper of **₹100 to ₹500** is highly recommended for commercial agreements like NDAs. Additionally, getting the document notarised by a local Notary Public adds an extra layer of authentication, preventing the receiving party from claiming their signature was forged.
