### The 11-Month Myth: The Secret Reason Behind the Tenure

Why is almost every rental contract you see in India executed for exactly 11 months? Is it a magical number required by law?

**The answer is financial and procedural, not mandatory.**

According to **Section 17(1)(d) of the Registration Act, 1908**, any lease of immovable property **from year to year, or for any term exceeding one year**, must be mandatorily registered at the local Sub-Registrar’s office. Registration involves paying substantial stamp duty and registration fees based on the total rent and deposit, plus multiple court visits.

To save both parties substantial time and money, Indian landlords and tenants generally execute an **11-month agreement**. Since the tenure is less than one year (12 months), mandatory registration under Section 17 is bypassed. Such an agreement can simply be executed on a non-judicial stamp paper of appropriate value and notarized, making it a faster, more convenient process while still remaining legally valid proof of the transaction.

*Pro Tip: If you need a long-term commercial lease (e.g., 5 or 9 years), you MUST opt for full mandatory registration at the Sub-Registrar’s office for complete legal protection.*

### Rent Agreement vs. Leave & License: Know the Distinction

Most people use these terms interchangeably, but they are fundamentally different legal concepts:

| Parameter | Standard Rent / Lease Agreement | Leave and License Agreement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Basis** | Transfer of Property Act, 1882 & State Rent Control Acts. | Indian Easements Act, 1882 (Sections 52-64). |
| **Nature of Right** | Creates a legal interest and right of "possession" over the property for the tenant. | Only grants a personal "permission" or "license" to use the premises. **No transfer of possession.** |
| **Eviction** | Extremely difficult. Tenants gain strong protection under state rent control laws against arbitrary eviction. | Very easy. Landlord can easily revoke the license and get back possession of the property. |
| **Which is better?** | Favors the **Tenant** by offering long-term security of tenure. | Strongly favors the **Landlord**, making it ideal for temporary residential/PG setups. |

### 8 Mandatory Clauses Every Agreement Must Contain

To prevent future deadlocks and financial disputes, your drafted agreement must clearly address these core elements:

1. **Detailed Property Description:** Write the exact Flat No, Floor, Building Name, Plot/Survey No, City, and PIN code.
2. **Deposit & Refund Terms:** The interest-free security deposit amount and, crucially, the timeframe for its refund (e.g., *"Deposit must be refunded within 7 days of peaceful handover, after standard wear-and-tear deductions"*).
3. **Lock-in Period:** A mandatory timeframe (typically 3 to 6 months) during which neither the landlord can evict nor the tenant can vacate without paying the remainder’s rent as a penalty.
4. **Notice Period for Termination:** Clearly state how many months' advance notice is required to terminate the agreement after the lock-in period (usually **1 or 2 months**).
5. **Annual Rent Escalation:** If you plan to renew, state the fixed percentage increase in rent (typically **5% to 10%**) to prevent arbitrary hikes later.
6. **Maintenance & Utility Charges:** Clarify who pays monthly housing society maintenance charges, municipal water bills, and electricity consumption.
7. **Restrictions on Use & Sub-Letting:** Explicitly prohibit illegal activities, severe structural modifications, and subletting any part of the premises to a third party without written consent.
8. **Fixture & Furniture Inventory (The Gold Rule):** Attach an annexure listing every fan, AC, geyser, sofa, bed, and RO purifier provided. Sign it jointly to prevent disputes over "damages" later.

> **5. LOCK-IN & TERMINATION:**
> Both parties agree to a mandatory **Lock-in Period of [e.g., 4] Months**. Neither party can terminate the agreement during the lock-in period. After the lock-in period, either party may terminate this agreement by giving **[e.g., 1 or 2] Months written notice** via official email or WhatsApp.

The Licensee shall not carry out any structural changes, commercial activities, or unlawful activities inside the premises.

### Mandatory Checklist Before Moving In

Drafting the document is only the first step. To make it a legally protected transaction, both parties must complete this checklist:

#### 1. Pay the Appropriate Stamp Duty
To be admissible in court, the agreement must be printed on non-judicial e-stamp paper. Stamp duty rates vary wildly by state and the total capital investment:
* **Delhi / NCR:** ₹50 or ₹100 e-Stamp Paper (depending on notarization).
* **Maharashtra:** Requires actual Leave & License registration (even for 11 months) or heavy scaled stamp duty. 
* **Karnataka (Bangalore):** Typically ₹200 e-Stamp Paper.

#### 2. Get it Notarized
After signing, visit a local courtside **Notary Public**. The Notary verifies identities and puts their official round stamp and signature on every page. This adds legal authentication to the agreement.

#### 3. Mandatory Tenant Police Verification (Landlord’s Shield)
This is not optional in most metro cities (like Mumbai, Delhi, and Noida). It is a criminal offense under Section 188 of the IPC (BNS) for a landlord to rent out a property without submitting a **Tenant Police Verification Form** (along with the tenant’s ID, native address proof, and passport-size photo) to the local police station. This protects the landlord if the tenant is later involved in criminal activities. Many states now allow this process **completely online** via the city police portal.
