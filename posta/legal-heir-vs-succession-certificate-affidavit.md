### The Biggest Indian Myth: "The Nominee Gets Everything"

Before we discuss certificates, we must address a dangerous misconception. Millions of Indians believe that if a father makes his eldest son the "Nominee" in his bank account, the son automatically becomes the absolute owner of that money after the father's death.

**The Supreme Court of India strongly disagrees.**

In landmark judgments, the Supreme Court has clarified the legal status of a Nominee:
* **A Nominee is merely a "Trustee" or a "Caretaker" of the funds.** 
* The bank’s only job is to hand over the money to the nominee so that the bank is free from liability. 
* However, the nominee does **NOT** become the owner of that money. The nominee is legally bound to distribute that money equally among all the rightful legal heirs (wife, sons, daughters) as per the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (or the respective personal law).

If you are not the sole legal heir, do not spend the nominee money thinking it is entirely yours. Your siblings can successfully sue you for their legal share.

### Legal Heir Certificate vs. Succession Certificate: The Ultimate Breakdown

When a person dies *Intestate* (without leaving a valid Will), you need official documentation to prove your relationship with the deceased. However, the document you need depends entirely on **what type of asset you are trying to claim.**

#### 1. The Legal Heir Certificate (Surviving Member Certificate)
* **Who issues it?** The local Revenue Officer, Tehsildar, or Mandal Revenue Officer (MRO).
* **What is it used for?** It is a simple administrative document used primarily to establish the relationship between the deceased and the surviving family members.
* **Where does it work?** It is used for transferring electricity connections, claiming compassionate appointments in government jobs, transferring PF/Gratuity, receiving government insurance schemes, and claiming small bank balances (usually below Rs. 1 Lakh to Rs. 5 Lakhs, depending on the bank's internal policy).
* **Where does it FAIL?** It is strictly **invalid** for selling real estate, transferring highly valued Demat shares, or claiming large, disputed bank deposits. 

#### 2. The Succession Certificate
* **Who issues it?** The District Civil Court or High Court, under the Indian Succession Act, 1925.
* **What is it used for?** It gives the holder the absolute legal authority to realize the debts and securities (bank balances, mutual funds, shares, bonds) of the deceased person.
* **Where does it work?** Banks, financial institutions, and stockbrokers are legally bound to accept a Succession Certificate for transferring high-value assets. If your father left behind Rs. 50 Lakhs in an FD without a nominee, the bank will strictly demand this certificate.
* **The Drawback:** It is a lengthy judicial process. You have to file a court petition, publish a notice in the newspaper, wait for objections for 45 days, and pay a heavy ad-valorem **Court Fee** (usually 2% to 4% of the total asset value) to the government.

### RBI Guidelines: Do You Always Need a Succession Certificate?

Banks often harass grieving families by demanding a Succession Certificate even for smaller amounts. You must know your rights under the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Master Circular on Customer Service**.

According to RBI guidelines:
* If the deceased account holder has registered a valid **Nominee**, the bank MUST release the funds to the nominee without asking for a Succession Certificate or Legal Heir Certificate (upon submitting the death certificate and KYC).
* For accounts **without a nominee**, banks are instructed to fix a threshold limit (usually up to Rs. 5 Lakhs). For amounts below this limit, banks should release the funds against a simple **Affidavit, Indemnity Bond, and a Letter of Disclaimer** from the legal heirs, without forcing the family to go to a civil court.

### Template: Affidavit for Legal Heirs (For Bank Claims & Tehsildar Applications)

If you are applying for a Legal Heir Certificate at the Tehsildar’s office, or claiming a smaller bank balance where the bank permits an affidavit, use this format. 

*Instructions: Print this on a Non-Judicial e-Stamp Paper (value Rs. 50 or Rs. 100 as per your state) and get it attested by a First Class Judicial Magistrate or a Notary Public.*

> **AFFIDAVIT OF SURVIVING LEGAL HEIRS**
>
> I, **[YOUR FULL NAME]**, S/o, D/o, W/o: Late **[Name of Deceased]**, aged about **[Age]** years, residing at **[Complete Residential Address]**, do hereby solemnly affirm and declare on oath as under:
>
> 1. That my father/mother/spouse, Late **[Name of Deceased]**, passed away on **[Date of Death]** at **[Place of Death, e.g., Apollo Hospital, Delhi]**.
> 2. That the deceased was a permanent resident of **[Deceased’s Complete Address]** at the time of their demise. A copy of the Death Certificate bearing Registration No. **[Certificate Number]** is annexed herewith.
> 3. That the deceased died intestate (without leaving any valid Will or testamentary disposition behind).
> 4. That the deceased left behind the following persons as their only surviving Class-I legal heirs as per the provisions of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956:
>
> | S.No | Name of the Legal Heir | Age | Relationship with the Deceased | Marital Status |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | 1. | [Name of Heir 1] | [Age] | [e.g., Wife / Widow] | Married |
> | 2. | [Your Name - Deponent] | [Age] | [e.g., Son] | Married |
> | 3. | [Name of Heir 3] | [Age] | [e.g., Daughter] | Unmarried |
>
> 5. That apart from the persons mentioned in the table above, there are no other legal heirs (adopted or otherwise) of the deceased. 
> 6. That this affidavit is being sworn in to be submitted before the **[Name of Authority, e.g., Tehsildar / Branch Manager of XYZ Bank]** for the purpose of obtaining a Legal Heir Certificate / claiming the funds lying in Savings Account No. **[Account Number]**.
> 7. That we, the aforementioned legal heirs, have no ongoing disputes among ourselves regarding the estate of the deceased.
>
> **DEPONENT**
> *(Signature)*
>
> **VERIFICATION:**
> I, the above-named deponent, do hereby verify that the facts stated in paragraphs 1 to 7 are true and correct to the best of my personal knowledge and belief. No part of it is false, and no material facts have been concealed therefrom.
>
> Verified at **[City Name]** on this **[Day]** day of **[Month], 20[Year]**.
>
> **DEPONENT**
> *(Signature)*

*(Note: Along with this affidavit, banks will usually ask the other siblings to sign a separate "Relinquishment Letter" or "No Objection Certificate" (NOC) in favor of the sibling who is receiving the funds).*

### 3 Steps to Avoid This Nightmare for Your Own Family

If reading about court fees, Tehsildars, and affidavits makes you anxious, you can save your children from this entire ordeal by doing three simple things today:

1. **Update Your Nominees:** Log into your net banking, mutual fund portals, and Demat accounts today. Ensure a valid nominee is updated everywhere. Do not leave the nominee field blank.
